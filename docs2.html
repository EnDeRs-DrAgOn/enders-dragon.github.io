16x16 Fairy Chess

PGN & FEN Reference Guide

1. PGN Move Structure

The notation system records every move using a custom format designed specifically for this 16×16 fairy chess variant.

12. (AP)l15l16=(Am)+ --XX--

A move can be broken down into several parts:

Part Meaning
12. The sequential move number.
(AP) The piece that moved. In this example, it is an Arrow Pawn.
l15 The square the piece started on.
l16 The square the piece moved to.
=(Am) The piece was promoted to an Amazonian.
+ The move gives check.
--XX-- The other side did not make a move for this turn. This is used in sandbox mode.

2. Basic Move

1. (P)k2k5 --XX--

This means:

  • (P) — A standard Pawn moved.
  • k2 — It started on k2.
  • k5 — It landed on k5.
  • --XX-- — No opposing move was recorded on this turn.

3. Captures

21. (A)i1xh2(A)h2 --XX--

The x indicates a capture.

The structure is:

(Piece)StartingSquare x LandingSquare (CapturedPiece)CapturedSquare

For a normal capture, the landing square and captured-piece square are the same.

(A)i1xh2(A)h2

Special pieces such as Arrow Pawns, Mortars, and Executioners can have different capture and landing locations. Those cases preserve both coordinates when necessary.

4. Promotions

14. (AP)l15l16=(Am)+ --XX--

Promotions are written directly after the move coordinates.

=(Piece)

For example:

  • =(A) — promoted to Amazon
  • =(R) — promoted to Rose
  • =(Ch) — promoted to Chancellor
  • =(Ab) — promoted to Archbishop
  • =(Ba) — promoted to Baronet
  • =(Ca) — promoted to Castle
  • =(E) — promoted to Elephant
  • =(Ex) — promoted to Executioner
  • =(ZN) — promoted to Zigzag Nightrider

5. Check, Checkmate & Stalemate

Special game-state symbols are placed immediately after the move (and after a promotion if one occurred).

20. (A)h14xh2(AP)h2+ --XX--

The symbols are:

Symbol Meaning
+ Check — the opponent's King is currently threatened.
# Checkmate — the opponent is in check and has no legal move.
% Stalemate — the opponent has no legal move, but is not in check.
22. (A)h2n8# --XX--

6. Sandbox vs Turn-Based Notation

Sandbox Mode

Every individual move receives its own sequential number. The unused side of the turn is represented by --XX--.

1. (P)k2k5 --XX-- 2. (P)k5k7 --XX-- 3. (P)k7k8 --XX-- 4. (P)k8k9 --XX--

Turn-Based Mode

White and Black share the same move number.

1. (P)k2k5 (P)k15k12 2. (P)k5k7 (P)k12k11 3. (P)k7k8 (P)k11k10

7. FEN Structure

The FEN system describes the entire current board position in a compact text format.

Your variant uses a modified FEN format consisting of:

BOARD_POSITION ACTIVE_SIDE

For example:

.../16/... w

The first part describes all 16 ranks of the board. The second part tells the game which side is currently active.

8. FEN Board Rows

Each rank is separated using a forward slash:

rank16/rank15/rank14/.../rank1

The board always contains 16 rows, corresponding to ranks 16 through 1.

Empty squares are represented using numbers.

16

This means that all 16 squares in that row are empty.

For example:

8P7

means:

  • 8 empty squares
  • 1 white standard Pawn
  • 7 empty squares

10. Multi-Character FEN Codes

Because several fairy pieces have names longer than one character, they are enclosed in parentheses.

(BP) (AP) (Ch) (ZN) (AfR) (AlR)

This prevents the FEN parser from confusing a multi-character piece with several separate pieces.

For example:

4(AP)5

means four empty squares, one Arrow Pawn, then five empty squares.

11. Active Side

The final character tells the engine whose turn it currently is.

w

means White to move.

b

means Black to move.

The active-side value is especially important when loading a FEN position into Turn-Based Mode.

12. Complete FEN Concept

A complete FEN therefore follows this general structure:

Rank 16 / Rank 15 / Rank 14 / ... / Rank 1 + active side

In actual input, the spaces around the slashes are omitted:

BOARD/ROWS/ARE/WRITTEN/HERE/UNTIL/16/ROWS/ARE/DEFINED w

Numbers represent consecutive empty squares, piece codes represent occupied squares, and the final w or b determines the active side.
